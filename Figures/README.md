Figures created during the development of the paper will be separated on two folders, one for the ones presented on the paper proper, and one with unused figures, which will be considered the Supplementary Material. Figures on Paper/ will be numbered as they appear on the paper:

- Paper/
  1. [Figure 1](https://github.com/RaphaLevy/Environmental_Drivers_of_Malaria/blob/main/Figures/Paper/total_active_infectious_cases_manaus_2017_2023.png): Total number of active infectious cases of malaria (_P. vivax_, SIVEP-Malaria) in rural Manaus, 2017-2023. The strong inter- and 
intra-annual variability of the disease burden is the central modelling challenge addressed in this work, and motivates the representation of all 
of the variability of the environmental drivers as well as the modifications to the baseline model.
  2. [Figure 2](https://github.com/RaphaLevy/Environmental_Drivers_of_Malaria/blob/main/Figures/Paper/weekly_beta_calibration_2017.png): Comparison between the observed active cases and the model estimation of the infectious human compartment $I_H$ for 2017.
  3. [Figure 3](https://github.com/RaphaLevy/Environmental_Drivers_of_Malaria/blob/main/Figures/Paper/coupled_and_human_Rt_variation_in_2017_with_months.png): Seasonal variation of the coupled and human basic reproduction number
$\mathcal{R}(t)$ for the calibrated model in 2017.
  4. [Figure 4](https://github.com/RaphaLevy/Environmental_Drivers_of_Malaria/blob/main/Figures/Paper/local_sensitivity_R0_IH.png): Normalized sensitivity index $S_x = \partial\log Y/\partial\log x$ of the 25 decomposed parameters (bars coloured by parameter group, as in Table 3), where $Y$ is the output being differentiated: $Y = \mathcal{R}\_{0}$ for the closed-form reproduction number in the left panel and $Y = Q\_{\mathrm{peak}}$ for the seasonal maximum of the infectious-human compartment of the calibrated model in the right panel. Left: closed-form reproduction number at the reference environmental state, by central differences with $h = 0.05$; the exact analytic values of Table 3 differ from these by at most $0.06$, the truncation error of the difference quotient. Right: seasonal maximum of the infectious-human compartment of the calibrated model, averaged over 2017-2023, same scheme. A positive index means the output increases with the parameter. The three _Anopheles_ suitability temperatures are exactly zero on the left because the thermal-suitability factor $f\_T$ is saturated at the reference climate.
  5. [Figure 5](https://github.com/RaphaLevy/Environmental_Drivers_of_Malaria/blob/main/Figures/Paper/sobol_ih_qpeak_trimmed.png): First-order ($S\_i$) and total-order ($S\_{T\_i}$) Sobol' indices of the decomposed parameters for the output $Y = Q\_{\mathrm{peak}}$, the seasonal peak of the infectious-human compartment defined in Eq. (39), over the whole admissible parameter box of Table A.3 (Saltelli design, $N = 512$; bars coloured by parameter group). Only the 11 parameters with non-negligible sensitivity are shown; the 14 parameters with total-order index below $2.7\times 10^{-3}$, from $T\_{\min}^{\mathrm{p}}$ to $T\_{\mathrm{opt}}^{\mathrm{p}}$, are omitted from the plot. Each parameter has two overlaid bars: the darker inner bar is the first-order index $S\_i$ and the lighter outer bar is the total-order index $S\_{T\_i}$. The total-order index also collects the variance that a parameter carries through its interactions with the remaining ones, hence $S\_{T\_i} \geq S\_i$. Since $Q\_{\mathrm{peak}}$ is a maximum, the $74.4\%$ of the sampled parameter sets that are locally subcritical leave it at the initial value $I\_H(0)$; the indices therefore characterize the sampled box and not the height of an established endemic wave.
  6. [Figure 6a](https://github.com/RaphaLevy/Environmental_Drivers_of_Malaria/blob/main/Figures/Paper/b1_eff_obs_vs_pred_all_years.png): Scatter plot of the estimated vs predicted weekly-free proportion of bites by susceptible mosquitoes on infected humans ($b_1$) that result in infection.
  7. [Figure 6b](https://github.com/RaphaLevy/Environmental_Drivers_of_Malaria/blob/main/Figures/Paper/b2_eff_obs_vs_pred_all_years.png): Scatter plot of the estimated vs predicted weekly-free proportion of bites by infected mosquitoes on susceptible humans ($b_2$) that result in infection.
  8. [Figure 7](https://github.com/RaphaLevy/Environmental_Drivers_of_Malaria/blob/main/Figures/Paper/env_response_b1_b2_combined.png): Partial dependence of the weekly free per-bite transmission probabilities $b_1$ and $b_2$ on each environmental predictor. Within each panel the upper and lower sub-panels share the $x$-axis and are drawn on their own $y$-scale. Each thin curve is one of the seven separately fitted per-year models (2017-2023), the thick curve is the across-year median and the shaded band spans the minimum-to-maximum range across the seven years. The $x$-axis covers the pooled observed range of each predictor over 2017-2023, so the curves are not extrapolated beyond the data; they are within-year partial dependences and not a fitted functional form.
